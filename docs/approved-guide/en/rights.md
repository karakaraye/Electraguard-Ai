# Your Rights as an Observer

As an accredited election observer, you play a vital role in supporting transparent and peaceful elections.

## What You Are Entitled to Do
- You have the right to watch the opening of the polling unit, voter accreditation, voting, sorting, counting, and the recording of results.
- You have the right to observe proceedings from a clear position where you can see what is happening without blocking voters or officials.
- You have the right to wear your official observer badge, lanyard, and identification at all times.
- You have the right to take factual notes and record times of key events.
- You have the right to speak peacefully with election officials when they are not actively attending to a voter.

## What You Must Not Do
- You must never touch ballot papers, ballot boxes, or official election materials.
- You must never tell voters how to vote or ask who they voted for.
- You must never wear party colors, party caps, or badges of any political party.
- You must never give instructions to election officials or interfere with their work.
- You must never declare results or make official statements on behalf of the electoral commission.

Local procedures and physical arrangements can differ between polling centers. If you are unsure of what is permitted at your assigned station, check with your coordinator.
